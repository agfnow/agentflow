# Clear answers and reports

Apply to all human-facing explanations, including chat, questions, updates, Replies, reports and documentation for users; preserve quoted input, exact identifiers and fixed record formats. **Human-facing explanations prioritize comprehension. Machine-only documents, including skill prompts, may be aggressively compressed; preserve requirements, exceptions, safeguards, precision and correctness. In mixed documents, apply each rule to its intended reader.** Owner language/style instructions prevail; this guide changes neither authority nor answer destinations.

**Highest human-facing writing goal — ultimate comprehension:** The intended reader must understand the essential result without guessing or opening supporting links. Easy reading and fast scanning are required: use short prose paragraphs or list items, one concept per block; long dense blocks fail even when complete. Brevity, formatting and terminology serve comprehension; never sacrifice meaning, necessary context, requirements, safeguards or decision-changing limits to them. Before every delivery, check from that reader’s knowledge: can they explain the result, who does what, why it matters and any decision/action needed? Include only applicable elements; clarify or rewrite until the answer is self-contained and unambiguous. This check grants no authority and requires no ritual, new document or owner confirmation.

- **Lead with the answer:** State the conclusion or recommendation and what the reader should do. Put any limit that changes that decision beside it. No setup story or mandatory summary label. For an unfamiliar choice, explain what happens and why it matters before the recommendation or question. Open every report with self-contained conclusions, key findings, applicable recommendations/decisions and material limits before chronology, evidence or detail; split rather than cut required content; keep the opening complete across short blocks. Postmortems/root-cause reports also put root causes and prevention recommendations in that opening.

- **Make scanning useful:** Put decisions/results before reasons, evidence and optional procedure. Use short prose for connected reasoning or list items for parallel/sequential points; neither format is mandatory. Keep each paragraph/item to one concept, usually 1–3 short sentences; split sooner at topic changes; never move a dense paragraph into one long bullet. Begin each block with its main point and separate blocks with blank lines. Headings help longer reports; avoid empty ceremony. When listing several document references (e.g. Sources), use a label with nested `-` list items, one per source line and blank-separated.

- **Speak plainly:** Write for an educated decision-maker who may not know the domain; respect their reasoning ability. Name concrete actions and their benefit. State the cause and consequence rather than only internal labels. Explain necessary jargon at first use in each independently read answer or report; remove unexplained abbreviations, vague qualifiers and strings of compressed clauses. Concise must still be understandable.

- **Editor links:** In saved Markdown, line links use `path#<line-number>`, never `path:<line-number>`; e.g. from `.agentflow/`: `Implemented [features.md](../skills/aglite/references/features.md#31)`. Resolve paths relative to the document; preserve named anchors for sections. In chat, follow host link requirements; otherwise use `#<line-number>` with verified checkout-root-relative paths, or absolute paths when the host cannot resolve relative targets. Never use report-relative `../` in chat. Wrap targets containing spaces in `<...>`.

- **Keep only useful detail:** Retain requirements, exceptions, safeguards and evidence needed to assess the answer. Link exact supporting records rather than repeat them. Keep workflow proof under [Round evidence](../SKILL.md#round-evidence), not in Reply unless explicitly requested; retain decision-changing limits in the answer. Move requested reproducible commands and specialist detail after the result; omit execution diaries and routine success summaries. At the authorized destination, keep the essential explanation in the answer so the reader need not open links to understand it.

- **Keep claims honest:** Separate observed results, assessment and unavailable proof. Include failures, limitations and next actions when they affect the request. Never shorten away a condition that changes authority or correctness.

- **Check before delivery:** Can the reader find the answer/action in the opening and scan paragraph/item starts for the rest? Split any long or multi-concept paragraph/item before delivery, preserving meaning and decision-changing limits. Can a reader unfamiliar with the domain explain what happens and why it matters? When asking them to decide, explain the options’ consequences and give any recommendation with its reason; separate independent choices from questions needed only for one option. Cut points that do not answer the request, affect their decision or support assessing it; link records instead of repeating them. Compare with the full request and surrounding rules for omissions/conflicts. Before asking for a consequential choice, including any choice changing an accepted outcome, guarantee or owner-chosen design, explain the requested/current approach and each proposed alternative in plain words: who does what, which capabilities remain, what changes or is lost, and how that affects the owner’s goal. Name any rule conflict without silently changing that goal; distinguish tested behavior from assumptions or illustrations. Obtain decisions on unresolved consequences, not unexplained labels; respect settled decisions and authority. If an unfamiliar reader cannot retell the work and consequence, rewrite before asking. Preserve required fields, notices, receipts and handoffs; writing style grants no new authority.
